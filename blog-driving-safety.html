Is it Safe to Drive in Morocco? What Tourists Need to Know

By Abdellah | Local Tour Expert | May 24, 2026

Morocco is an incredibly beautiful country to explore by road. Driving through the winding roads of the High Atlas Mountains or cruising past the endless palm groves of the south is an unforgettable experience.

But if you are planning your trip, you are probably wondering: "Is it actually safe for a tourist to drive in Morocco?"

As a local Moroccan driver and tour guide, I want to give you the honest truth. Yes, it is safe, but it is definitely not like driving in North America or Europe. Here is what you need to know before you rent a car.

1. The Cities Can Be Chaotic

Driving in the countryside is peaceful, but navigating cities like Marrakech, Casablanca, or Fes can be very stressful. You will share the road with donkeys, thousands of motorbikes, pedestrians crossing everywhere, and drivers who treat lanes as "suggestions." You have to be highly alert 100% of the time.

2. Police Checkpoints are Everywhere

Morocco is very safe, partly because there is a strong police presence on the roads. You will see police checkpoints at the entrance and exit of almost every town. Always slow down to a crawl. Wait for the officer to physically wave you through before driving past. If you don't wait, you will be fined!

3. Night Driving is Not Recommended

I always tell my clients to avoid driving at night outside the cities. The rural roads are often unlit, and it is very common to encounter sheep, dogs, or people walking on the side of the road in pitch darkness.

4. Beware of Speed Traps

The speed limits in Morocco change very suddenly. You can be driving 100 km/h and suddenly hit a 60 km/h sign hidden behind a tree. Police use radar guns frequently, and fines must be paid in cash on the spot.
