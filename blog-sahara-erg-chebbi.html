Erg Chebbi vs Erg Chigaga: Which Sahara Dunes to Visit?

Erg Chebbi or Erg Chigaga? Height, access and honesty — pick your dunes.

← All guides

If you're planning a Sahara desert trip from Marrakech, you'll quickly encounter two names: Erg Chebbi (near Merzouga) and Erg Chigaga (near Zagora). Both offer incredible desert experiences, but they're quite different. I've guided tours to both deserts for years. Here's my honest comparison.

Erg Chebbi (Merzouga) – The Popular Choice

Erg Chebbi is Morocco's most famous dune field, near the village of Merzouga. These massive golden dunes rise up to 150 meters and stretch for about 50 kilometers. Most travelers choose Merzouga for their first Sahara experience — and for good reason.

✅ Pros:

  • ✓ Spectacular high dunes — incredibly photogenic
  • ✓ Easy access — paved road to Merzouga village
  • ✓ Many camp options (basic to luxury)
  • ✓ Camel trekking from the village

❌ Cons:

  • ✗ More tourists in peak season
  • ✗ Some areas commercialized

Erg Chigaga (Zagora) – The Wild Alternative

Erg Chigaga is located near Zagora, about 7 hours from Marrakech. The dunes are slightly smaller but spread over a vast area. The last 2 hours are on rough desert tracks — the journey itself is an adventure.

✅ Pros:

  • ✓ More remote and wild — fewer tourists
  • ✓ True desert solitude experience
  • ✓ Off-road adventure to reach it

❌ Cons:

  • ✗ Harder to reach — 4x4 required
  • ✗ Fewer camp options
  • ✗ Dunes not as high as Erg Chebbi

Our Recommendation

For 95% of travelers, Erg Chebbi (Merzouga) is the better choice. The dunes are more spectacular, access is easier, and you get the full Sahara experience. That's why we specialize in Merzouga tours. If you're interested in Zagora/Erg Chigaga, contact us for private custom tours.

Practical notes

  • Chebbi needs 3 days from Marrakech minimum; Chigaga needs 4+.
  • Desert winter nights drop near freezing — camps provide blankets, bring layers anyway.
  • Summer midday heat is extreme; travel shoulder seasons if you can.
  • Sand gets everywhere: dry bags and sealed pouches save cameras and phones.
