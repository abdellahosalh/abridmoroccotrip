Agoudal: the High Atlas Village That Fell From the Sky

East of Imilchil, a remote high village sits beside an officially recognised iron meteorite fall. This is Agoudal — and the road that leads there.

← All guides

Few villages on earth can say that part of them came from space. Agoudal, a high settlement in Morocco's eastern High Atlas, gives its name to the Agoudal iron meteorite — an officially recognised fall whose fragments local people were collecting and selling to travellers years before scientists confirmed what they were.

Where exactly is Agoudal?

Agoudal lies east of Imilchil, on the high mountain road that continues toward Amouguer and Rich. The meteorite strewn field — the ground over which fragments scattered — sits about 20 km southeast of Imilchil and only some 4 km west of the village itself, crossed in its northern part by the high R704 mountain road. The village is a working high-altitude settlement of fields, irrigation channels and stone houses, not a tourist stop: most travellers pass within a few kilometres of it without ever knowing what lies in the soil around them.

The meteorite story, briefly

Two small pieces of iron picked up near Agoudal in 2000 were sold to tourists as curiosities. In 2011, another piece reached a dealer in the town of Rich — Er-Rich, at the eastern end of our route — who recognised it as a genuine iron meteorite. Scientific analysis followed, and in 2013 the find was officially approved under the name Agoudal and classified as a IIAB iron. Traded pieces are still often sold under the commercial name "Imilchil". Later fieldwork identified a small, eroded impact structure within the strewn field, listed in the international Earth Impact Database with an estimated age of around 105,000 years.

The stones were souvenirs first and science second — the villagers knew the strange heavy iron long before the laboratories did.

The road to Agoudal: the Assif Melloul valley

Almost nobody visits Agoudal on its own; the experience is the approach. Leaving Imilchil eastward, the road follows the Assif Melloul — the "white river" — into a valley of cliffs, caves and terraced gardens. An easy guided walk of about an hour leads into the gorges, then the track climbs on through high pastoral country to the village. Progress is slow, the views are constant, and the silence — broken by wind, water and sheep bells — is the point.

Village nights in Agoudal

There are no hotels here — travellers sleep in a village gîte, eat what the household cooks, and learn quickly how cold a High Atlas night can be outside summer. It is the simplest overnight of our Azilal-to-Rich traverse, and the one most travellers remember best.

Practical notes for Agoudal

  • Season: late spring to early autumn. Winter can close the high sections with snow.
  • Road: high mountain track in places — go with a local driver, not self-drive.
  • Bring: warm layers even in summer, sturdy shoes, cash (no ATMs, no card payments).
  • Meteorite hunting: the strewn field has been thoroughly searched for years; go for the story and the landscape, not for finds.
