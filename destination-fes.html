Fes – Capital of the Imperial North

The oldest of Morocco’s imperial cities — a nine-thousand-street medieval medina, tanneries, madrasas and craft traditions that have not changed for centuries.

RegionNorthern Morocco
Best timeMar–May & Sep–Nov
IconMedina of Fes el-Bali (UNESCO)
Part ofThe Imperial Cities circuit

Founded in 789, Fes is Morocco’s spiritual and cultural heart. Inside its ancient walls you will find the shopfronts of the Chaouwara tanneries with their rainbow vats of dye, the extraordinary tilework of the Bou Inania Madrasa, the world’s oldest university at Al-Qarawiyyin, and medina lanes so narrow that everything is still moved by hand or donkey.

The city sits naturally in a north-Morocco circuit that takes in the imperial capitals Rabat and Meknes, the blue town of Chefchaouen and the Roman ruins of Volubilis — all of which we combine in our multi-day circuits.

“In Fes you do not walk through history — you swim in it. The smell of the tanneries, the call of the sellers, the tilework that took generations.” — Abdellah, founder & Berber guide

Highlights

Chouwara Tanneries

Medieval leather dyeing in stone vats — the unmissable view from the terraces above.

Bou Inania Madrasa

A school of cedar, marble and zellige — still one of the finest interiors in Morocco.

Medina of Fes el-Bali

The largest car-free urban zone in the world — get lost on purpose, with a guide.

Volubilis & Meknes

Roman mosaics and a grand imperial city best seen together on a day trip.

Good to know

Do I need a guide in the Fes medina?+

Strongly recommended — the streets form a maze and the attractions are spread through them. A local guide turns it from confusing to captivating.

How many days do I need in Fes?+

Two days is comfortable: one in the old medina and one for the tanneries, palaces and a trip to Volubilis and Meknes.

Where does a Fes visit fit in a Morocco route?+

Fes links the north with the desert — the classic long circuits arrive from Chefchaouen or the Imperial Cities and continue south to the Sahara.
